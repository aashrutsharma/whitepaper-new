<Info>
These ideas come up again and again across these docs. Read together, they explain why Superadmission is built the way it is.
</Info>

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="1. Use what India already built" icon="blocks">
    Superadmission does not invent another student ID. It anchors a profile to <Tooltip tip="APAAR is the Automated Permanent Academic Account Registry. It is a single academic ID that follows a student through school and every degree after it.">APAAR</Tooltip>. It fetches documents from DigiLocker. It checks identity through Aadhaar. It follows the <Tooltip tip="The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, is India's data protection law.">DPDP Act</Tooltip>.
  </Card>
  <Card title="2. The authority decides" icon="landmark">
    The authority sets the rules, reviews the result and decides when to publish. Superadmission runs the process the authority already owns. It does not take over the decision.
  </Card>
  <Card title="3. A person signs off on anything that decides a seat" icon="user-check">
    If a decision could change whether a student gets a seat, a person reviews it before it becomes final.
  </Card>
  <Card title="4. Show the chances, never choose" icon="lightbulb">
    PraveshAI shows a band next to each choice. It never changes, reorders or locks in a student's list.
  </Card>
  <Card title="5. Stay neutral" icon="scale">
    Manifest borrows an idea from UPI and ONDC: shared infrastructure, not a product competing for attention. No institution can pay to look better.
  </Card>
  <Card title="6. Say what is not settled" icon="info">
    These docs list open questions plainly, instead of hiding them. Some things depend on approvals that are not in Superadmission's hands.
  </Card>
  <Card title="7. Go one step at a time" icon="route">
    Each new connection goes live only after its own pilot works.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>
