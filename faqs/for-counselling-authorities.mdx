<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What can a counselling authority do on Superadmission?" icon="landmark">
    A counselling authority, or any body that conducts admissions, can set up a round and run seat allocation. It can then review the results and publish them. All of this happens through the Superadmission Admin Portal.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How does Superadmission help counselling bodies?" icon="handshake">
    It helps in three ways. It runs the allocation algorithm and allocates seats. It verifies documents through <Tooltip tip="DigiLocker is the government's digital document locker. Boards and universities place signed, verified copies of certificates there.">DigiLocker</Tooltip> or manual review. It also answers and sorts grievances. Together, this means less manual work for the authority.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What stays in the authority's hands?" icon="list-checks">
    The authority alone sets the eligibility rules, and they are applied exactly as configured during seat allocation. It also sets the reservation mandates, category percentages and quotas, which are its own policy. It sets the seat matrix too. Finally, it decides when to run rounds and publish results.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why is there less manual work?" icon="file-check">
    Many documents arrive pre-verified, so there is less to check. Documents that the platform still marks for human review come with notes on what looks unclear or incorrect. Reviewers do not have to start from scratch.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Will there be fewer disputes?" icon="scale">
    Yes, that is the aim. The system removes much of the confusion that causes student errors, so fewer disputes begin in the first place. An assisted grievance management system is also being built, to handle and sort the grievances that do come in.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What can the authority see while a round is running?" icon="activity">
    The portal gives real-time updates and a live view of the round. Through graphs, it shows how many seats are filling under each program and category. It also shows the students' acceptance rate and the time taken across the process.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="When does the authority get an alert?" icon="bell">
    An alert is sent when something needs attention. For example, when an allocation run fails, or when the verification queue backs up. It is also sent when a grievance is urgent or its <Tooltip tip="A service level agreement is the time limit within which a grievance must be answered or solved.">SLA</Tooltip> is about to expire.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can the authority extend a deadline?" icon="calendar-clock">
    Yes. If a deadline is near and many registered students have not yet logged their choices, the authority sees it ahead of time. It can then choose to extend the deadline if required.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can the authority compare different rounds?" icon="trending-up">
    Yes. The authority can see and compare different rounds of allocation dynamically through the portal. This view sits alongside the live view of the round that is running.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Will every action be recorded?" icon="file-clock">
    That is one of our aims. We want authorities and the government to have clearer operational workflows and better access to relevant information. We also want an immutable and detailed record of every action, and admission processes that are easy to configure.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
